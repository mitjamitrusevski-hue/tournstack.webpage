Why we’re building TournStack

The registration details, guest requirements and browser experience that led us to build our own tournament software.

Mitja Mitruševski6 Nov 2026 · 4 min read

Published by TournStack

Key takeaways
  • We needed shirt-size selection at registration, a prize draw for players and guests, and an interactive event widget.
  • The apps we assessed did not meet our guest requirement; we also wanted people to join through a browser without downloading an app.
  • In our own use, people without extensive knowledge of the sport or its rules handled their tasks before and during the event without problems.
  • Judge an administrative improvement by the event-day task it frees you to focus on.
  • TournStack remains in development, informed by early live use in Lisbon and work towards further pilots.

We started building TournStack after looking for tournament management software that would fit our own events. The options we assessed covered parts of what we needed, but something was always missing: a registration detail, a way to include guests, or a tool that connected with the rest of the event.

Those details mattered because our events involved more than the players on court. Guests came to support them, people needed practical information, and we wanted the organising tools to support that whole experience.

What we needed for our events

Our requirements included collecting shirt sizes during registration, running a prize draw for players, guests attending the event, or both, and using an interactive event widget on our website. We wanted these details to fit into the same organising process.

At the time, registrations came through Google Forms and Sheets. We then moved contacts into email lists and separate event and prize-draw tools. Player lists, guest lists, waiting lists and jersey sizes all needed checking.

The work continued whenever information changed. A correction in one place could mean another list needed updating, and we had to keep track of which information was current.

That shaped how we assessed tournament registration software. We were looking at the practical sequence from registration to event day, including the smaller details that our events required.

Why guests and browser access mattered

Guests were an important part of that sequence. People could attend without playing, and we needed the software to account for them. Among the apps we assessed, we did not find one that met our guest requirement.

We also wanted players and guests to participate through a browser. Our preference was for someone to follow a link, register or find event information without having to download an app first.

A player may need registration details or a match time. A guest may want to know where to go or follow the results. We wanted the information and participation options to suit those different roles.

Why we decided to build

The combination of those requirements led us to start TournStack. We wanted player and guest registration, event information and prize draws to connect, with fewer separate updates for the organiser to manage.

While we were building the app, many of the people involved did not have extensive knowledge of the sport or its rules. We needed the tools to help them understand what to do, without assuming that background.

In our own use, those people were able to carry out their tasks before and on the day of the event without problems. That experience reinforced the importance of clear guidance throughout preparation and event day.

We are continuing to develop tournament and league tools around that experience. Guided setup should explain the choices an organiser needs to make, while connected event information should reduce the need to copy the same details between places.

The organiser still needs to review information and make decisions during the event. Our aim is to make that work clearer and easier to follow. Our vision for TournStack explains the broader direction behind those choices.

What better tools should make room for

The useful result of improving administration depends on the event. It might mean more attention for arriving players, a clearer briefing for volunteers or time to watch a match you helped organise.

A practical place to start is to name one part of your next event where you would like to be more present. Then identify a recurring task that competes for that attention.

For example, you might want to welcome newcomers but expect to spend that time checking several versions of a registration list. That gives you a specific process to examine: where information is collected, who updates it and who needs the final version.

The improvement can then be judged against something concrete. Does the process leave fewer details to reconcile? Can the people helping you find what they need? That is useful whether you change software or improve how you use the tools already available.

What happens next

The core system had an early live use in Lisbon at the Timeless Passion Padel Tournament on 28 September 2026. We are using that experience as we refine TournStack for further pilots with sports organisers.

TournStack remains in development. Setup, event control and support still need testing and improvement, and we will confirm the available sports, formats and features for each pilot or release.

If you organise tournaments or leagues and recognise some of these needs, tell us about your event. We can explain the current development scope and discuss whether a pilot could fit your situation.
