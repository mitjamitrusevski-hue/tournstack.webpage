Meet Coach Niko and the people building TournStack

Meet our fictional sporting host and the people behind him. Find out what to ask Niko, when to contact the team and how to join the conversation.

Mitja Mitruševski27 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

Published by TournStack

Key takeaways
  • Coach Niko is a fictional host and AI assistant; his Coach title is not a real qualification.
  • Mitja Mitruševski and Jane Stoimanov remain responsible for TournStack’s direction and commitments.
  • Use the contact form for a human enquiry; the chat does not automatically forward your conversation.
  • Community participation is welcome even if another tool works better for your event.

Coach Niko Stackington is TournStack’s fictional sporting host and the character behind our public AI assistant. He gives visitors a friendly place to ask a question. Mitja Mitruševski and Jane Stoimanov are the people responsible for the project behind him.

Picture a community hockey evening: players chasing the puck, friends following from behind the glass, someone watching their first match. People bring different levels of experience to the same rink. We want conversations around TournStack to leave room for that variety too.

Who is Coach Niko?

Niko is an approachable character with an interest in sport and the people who organise it. His role is to explain, welcome questions and help a visitor find a useful next step.

The Coach name describes that encouraging role. He is fictional; it does not give him a real coaching qualification or authority over officials. His public chat is an AI assistant, so an answer can be incomplete or mistaken. A confident sporting voice is no substitute for the relevant source.

Niko has a fictional backstory as well as a name. His Slovenian sporting character wears number 42, a reference to The Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy. Those details belong to the mascot’s story. They are not a real employee biography, a sporting record or evidence for an answer he gives.

That distinction matters when you meet him in different places: the character can be familiar, while the person or service speaking through him needs to stay clear.

What is useful to ask him?

Ask Niko for an explanation of TournStack or a sporting concept, with enough context to make the question clear. He works from the public information available to the assistant and should say when a detail needs confirmation.

For a hypothetical hockey example, a spectator might ask how the group stage differs from a knockout stage. That is a request for an explanation. Asking whether a particular team has qualified requires the event’s confirmed results and competition rules. Niko’s explanation cannot replace those records or the organiser’s decision.

You can also ask what a TournStack term means or where to learn more about the project. For current availability, pilot requirements or a commitment about your event, speak with the team. The hockey setting here is illustrative; it does not announce a hockey release.

Who is building TournStack?

Mitja Mitruševski is our Vision & Commercial Lead. Jane Stoimanov is our R&D & Technology Lead. Together, they are responsible for the project’s direction, development and the commitments made in its name.

Niko helps communicate that work. Decisions about the product, partnerships and what we can deliver remain with people. TournStack is still in development, and the team confirms the scope of each pilot.

Our earlier article explains why we started building TournStack. Here, the useful distinction is who can explain an idea and who can take responsibility for a specific answer about the project.

When should a question go to the team?

Contact us when you want to discuss an event, a commercial question or a possible collaboration. Describe the sport, the occasion and what you want to achieve. That gives us somewhere concrete to start without needing participant lists or private information about players.

The contact form is the route to the human team. Submit the message yourself: asking Niko to contact us does not transfer the chat to a person or automatically forward the conversation. Include the context you want us to receive.

For a decision about an event already taking place, go to its organiser or responsible official. Niko cannot change a score, register a player or settle an event dispute.

A community with room for different experiences

We want the TournStack community on Reddit to be useful to people who organise, play and follow sport. Niko’s presence there is team-operated, with human moderation and a clear TournStack affiliation.

You are welcome if another application works well for your club, or if you manage an event with simpler tools. An account of what worked, a thoughtful question or a different view can make a discussion worthwhile without becoming a request to buy something.

The Reddit account and the website assistant have different roles. A post from the team is a public contribution to the forum; the website chat is an AI conversation. Neither replaces a private enquiry submitted through the contact form. Keep personal event details out of a public thread, and choose the contact route when the team needs to consider your situation privately.

Come with a question or a point of view

Meet Niko through the website assistant, join a community discussion, or contact the team about your event. Choose the route that fits what you need. There is room for a first question, an experienced organiser’s view and the people who come simply to enjoy the game.
